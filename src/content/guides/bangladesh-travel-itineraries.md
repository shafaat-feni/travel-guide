# Bangladesh Travel Itineraries: 5, 7, 10 and 14 Days

Planning a trip to Bangladesh is easier when you choose a manageable route before booking transport and accommodation. The country offers several very different experiences: the busy streets and historic neighbourhoods of **Dhaka**, tea gardens and forest walks around **Srimangal**, the river landscapes of **Sylhet**, the long sandy coast at **Cox’s Bazar**, guided boat journeys into the **Sundarbans**, and archaeological sites such as **Paharpur** and **Bagerhat**.

The main challenge is not a lack of places to visit. It is the time needed to move between them. Road journeys can be slower than a map suggests, domestic flights do not always fit neatly with international connections, and some nature-based trips require advance arrangements. A good Bangladesh itinerary leaves room for travel, rest and unexpected changes instead of trying to cover every region in one visit.

This guide provides practical routes for **5, 7, 10 and 14 days**, explains which destinations work well together, and helps you choose a plan that suits your interests. For entry requirements and arrival planning, start with our [Bangladesh Visa Guide](/plan-your-trip/bangladesh-visa/) and [Dhaka Airport Arrival Guide](/plan-your-trip/dhaka-airport-arrival-guide/).

## Choose the Right Bangladesh Itinerary

| Trip length | Best approach | A realistic focus |
|---|---|---|
| 5 days | Keep travel distances short | Dhaka and nearby heritage, or Dhaka plus one destination by air |
| 7 days | Combine the capital with one region | Dhaka and Sylhet/Srimangal, or Dhaka and Cox’s Bazar |
| 10 days | Explore two regions at a measured pace | Dhaka, one nature or coastal region, and selected heritage stops |
| 14 days | Build a wider route with travel buffers | Dhaka, the northeast, and either the coast or a planned Sundarbans expedition |

**Important:** These are suggested structures, not fixed tour schedules. Transport frequency, attraction opening times, weather, public holidays and current security advice can affect the best route. Confirm arrangements for your dates before paying for non-refundable bookings.

## Before You Choose a Route

### Start and finish in Dhaka

Most international visitors arrive at Hazrat Shahjalal International Airport in Dhaka. Unless your ticket allows a convenient international arrival or departure elsewhere, plan your route around Dhaka and avoid assuming that a domestic flight will connect smoothly with an international flight on the same day.

If you are departing Bangladesh on a long-haul flight, it is prudent to return to Dhaka at least the day before departure when travelling from another region. A same-day connection may be possible in some circumstances, but road delays, weather, schedule changes or a missed domestic connection can make it risky.

### Choose destinations that fit together geographically

- **Dhaka and Sonargaon:** A useful pairing for city history and a day trip outside the capital.
- **Srimangal and Sylhet:** A natural combination for tea gardens, forest trails, rivers and other northeastern landscapes. Allow enough time for transfers between the two.
- **Cox’s Bazar and Chattogram city:** Can be combined if you have enough days, but they are separate stops and traffic or transport schedules matter.
- **Sundarbans and Bagerhat:** A logical southwestern heritage-and-nature combination, although a Sundarbans trip needs advance planning and should not be treated as a simple independent day trip.
- **Paharpur and other northern heritage sites:** Often need more overland time than first-time visitors expect. Add them to a longer itinerary rather than squeezing them between distant regions.

For detailed destination information, see our [Dhaka Travel Guide](/destinations/dhaka-travel-guide/), [Cox’s Bazar Travel Guide](/destinations/cox-s-bazar-travel-guide/), [Srimangal Travel Guide](/destinations/srimangal-travel-guide/), [Sylhet Travel Guide](/destinations/sylhet-travel-guide/) and [Sundarbans Travel Guide](/destinations/sundarbans-travel-guide/).

## 5-Day Bangladesh Itinerary: Dhaka and Nearby Heritage

**Best for:** Visitors with limited leave, travellers who prefer not to take another domestic flight, and people who want an introduction to Bangladeshi history, food and everyday city life.

### Day 1 — Arrive in Dhaka

Arrive, transfer to your accommodation and keep the first day flexible. Immigration, baggage collection, traffic and jet lag may take more time than expected. If you arrive early and feel rested, explore the area around your hotel or take a short, low-pressure walk rather than booking a packed sightseeing schedule.

### Day 2 — Explore Old Dhaka

Set aside most of the day for selected sights in Old Dhaka. Depending on opening times and traffic, options may include **Ahsan Manzil**, **Lalbagh Fort**, historic streets, markets and the riverfront around **Sadarghat**. These places are not all next door to one another, and Old Dhaka can be congested; choose a manageable cluster and allow time to eat and rest.

A riverfront visit can be interesting, but use common sense around busy landing points and follow local safety advice. Do not assume that every site will be open on the day you arrive.

### Day 3 — Dhaka’s museums, architecture and modern city

Choose a few places that match your interests, such as the **National Parliament area**, museums, cultural institutions or the University of Dhaka neighbourhood. Check visitor access and opening arrangements in advance, especially for official buildings where entry may be restricted. Use the afternoon for a relaxed meal or market visit instead of crossing the city repeatedly.

### Day 4 — Day trip to Sonargaon

Travel to **Sonargaon**, the historic settlement associated with Bengal’s past, to visit the Bangladesh Folk Art and Crafts Foundation when open and explore the historic buildings of **Panam Nagar** where access is permitted. Check current opening arrangements before leaving Dhaka and allow a buffer for road traffic in both directions.

### Day 5 — Flexible morning and departure

Keep the final day light. If your international flight is later, use the time for breakfast, a nearby attraction or last-minute shopping. Do not plan a long excursion on the day of departure. If your flight leaves early, you may need to move the shopping or sightseeing to Day 3.

**Alternative for beach lovers:** If Cox’s Bazar is a priority, replace the Sonargaon day and some Dhaka sightseeing with a flight-based coastal stay. This option is less flexible and depends on domestic flight schedules, airport transfers and your international departure time. For only five days, do not try to fit Dhaka, Cox’s Bazar, Sylhet and the Sundarbans into one trip.

## 7-Day Bangladesh Itinerary: Dhaka and the Northeast

**Best for:** Visitors interested in a mix of urban culture, tea gardens, forest walks and river scenery.

### Day 1 — Arrive in Dhaka

Arrive and settle into your accommodation. Keep sightseeing optional, especially after a long international journey. Read our [Dhaka Airport Arrival Guide](/plan-your-trip/dhaka-airport-arrival-guide/) for practical arrival planning.

### Day 2 — Dhaka highlights

Spend the day on a carefully selected route through Old Dhaka or a mix of historic and modern city sights. Rather than trying to see every major landmark, choose a few places and leave enough time for meals and traffic. If you prefer a slower start, move one of the Day 2 activities to the final Dhaka day.

### Day 3 — Travel to Srimangal

Travel to **Srimangal**, known for its tea-growing landscape. You may be able to travel by train, bus or private vehicle depending on the service and your preferred comfort level. Check schedules and book through reliable channels. Do not assume that every train or bus operates at a convenient time for your plan.

After arrival, keep the afternoon easy: check in, visit a tea garden where visitors are permitted, or arrange a local guide for the following day.

### Day 4 — Tea gardens and Lawachara National Park

Explore the tea-growing countryside and consider a guided visit to **Lawachara National Park**. Stay on authorised paths, follow park rules and keep noise low to avoid disturbing wildlife. Seeing wild animals is never guaranteed. If you want to visit a nearby community, arrange the visit respectfully and ask before taking photographs of people or homes.

### Day 5 — Transfer to Sylhet

Travel to **Sylhet** and use the rest of the day for a relaxed city visit, local food or a religious and cultural landmark such as the shrine area associated with Hazrat Shah Jalal. Keep the plan flexible because transfer time varies.

### Day 6 — Choose one Sylhet-area excursion

Choose one main excursion rather than trying to visit several widely separated places. Depending on weather, road conditions and local access, possibilities include **Jaflong**, **Lalakhal**, **Ratargul Swamp Forest** or another destination listed by the Bangladesh Tourism Board. Boat trips depend on water level, weather and local arrangements; use reputable operators and follow safety instructions.

During heavy rain or periods of flooding, some routes and outdoor activities may be disrupted. Check local conditions before setting out.

### Day 7 — Return to Dhaka and depart only if timing allows

Return to Dhaka by a suitable flight or overland service. If you have an international flight on Day 7, this plan is tight: a delay could cause you to miss it. The safer version is to return to Dhaka on Day 6 and use Day 7 for departure. If your international ticket allows it, add an eighth day rather than relying on a same-day connection.

**Why this route works:** Srimangal and Sylhet offer related but distinct experiences, and the trip avoids adding another distant region. It is still important to check transport times before finalising the order.

## 7-Day Alternative: Dhaka and Cox’s Bazar

**Best for:** Travellers who want the capital, Bangladeshi food and culture, and time by the Bay of Bengal rather than a multi-stop nature itinerary.

- **Day 1:** Arrive in Dhaka; rest and prepare for the domestic journey.
- **Day 2:** Visit selected Dhaka highlights. If you have an early flight the next morning, stay somewhere with practical airport access.
- **Day 3:** Fly or travel to **Cox’s Bazar**, then check in and visit a nearby part of the waterfront if time permits.
- **Day 4:** Explore Cox’s Bazar at a relaxed pace. Check local beach conditions and follow safety warnings; do not assume every beach area is safe for swimming.
- **Day 5:** Consider a day trip along the coastal road towards **Himchari** and **Inani**, allowing plenty of time for traffic and stops. Confirm current access and weather before leaving.
- **Day 6:** Keep this as a flexible beach or rest day, or return to Dhaka depending on your flight schedule.
- **Day 7:** Depart from Dhaka if you returned the previous day; otherwise, use this as a buffer rather than depending on a same-day domestic-to-international connection.

For this itinerary, check the [Cox’s Bazar Travel Guide](/destinations/cox-s-bazar-travel-guide/) and [Transport in Bangladesh](/plan-your-trip/transport-in-bangladesh/). Do not add the Chattogram Hill Tracts simply to fill a day; check current government travel advisories before considering travel to that region.

## 10-Day Bangladesh Itinerary: Dhaka, the Northeast and a Buffer Day

**Best for:** Travellers who want more than one region without turning the entire holiday into transport days.

This itinerary prioritises Dhaka and the northeast. It is generally easier to manage than combining the northeast, Cox’s Bazar and the Sundarbans in only ten days.

- **Days 1–2 — Dhaka:** Arrive, recover from the journey and visit a selection of Old Dhaka, museums, architecture, markets or cultural sites. Group stops by area.
- **Day 3 — Travel to Srimangal:** Make the journey and keep the afternoon light.
- **Day 4 — Srimangal:** Explore tea gardens and, if conditions and access allow, visit Lawachara National Park with a responsible guide.
- **Day 5 — Transfer to Sylhet:** Travel to Sylhet and explore nearby sights at a relaxed pace.
- **Day 6 — Sylhet-area excursion:** Choose one principal excursion, such as Ratargul, Jaflong or Lalakhal, based on season, water conditions and access.
- **Day 7 — Flexible northeast day:** Use this for a second excursion, a slower day or recovery from rain-related disruption. Avoid treating every named attraction as a guaranteed stop.
- **Day 8 — Return to Dhaka:** Travel back and stay overnight.
- **Day 9 — Dhaka or nearby heritage:** Use this as a flexible day for a museum, shopping, a missed city attraction or Sonargaon if transport and opening times make it practical.
- **Day 10 — Departure:** Keep the day for your international flight.

### Want the Sundarbans instead?

For a trip focused on wildlife and waterways, consider replacing the northeast section with a planned Sundarbans expedition. The **Sundarbans is a UNESCO World Heritage property**, but visiting the forest is not equivalent to visiting an ordinary city attraction. Boat arrangements, authorised access, permits and the operator’s itinerary need to be checked ahead of time. Do not enter the forest independently or spend the night there without the required approval. The Bangladesh Forest Department’s [Sundarbans tourism portal](https://sundarbantourism.bforest.gov.bd/en/one-day-tour-ticket){target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} explains the official tour-ticket process and visitor rules.

Allow the number of days specified by your chosen licensed operator, plus travel time to and from the embarkation point. Do not assume a one-day ticket covers a multi-day cruise or that every cruise visits the same locations.

## 14-Day Bangladesh Itinerary: A Broader First Visit

**Best for:** Visitors with two weeks who want a varied trip and can accept several travel days. This route combines the capital, the northeast and the coast while protecting time for the international departure.

- **Days 1–2 — Dhaka:** Arrive, rest and explore selected historic and cultural areas. Leave enough time for Old Dhaka rather than trying to see the whole city in one day.
- **Day 3 — Sonargaon or a slower Dhaka day:** Choose a day trip to Sonargaon if opening arrangements and road conditions suit, or stay in Dhaka for museums, markets and food.
- **Day 4 — Travel to Srimangal:** Travel to the tea region and keep the afternoon free.
- **Days 5–6 — Srimangal:** Visit tea gardens and plan a nature outing, such as Lawachara National Park, subject to opening arrangements, weather and local guidance.
- **Day 7 — Travel to Sylhet:** Transfer to Sylhet, with a relaxed evening.
- **Day 8 — Sylhet-area excursion:** Choose one major destination based on conditions and your interests.
- **Day 9 — Return to Dhaka:** Use a suitable flight or overland service. Keep the evening unplanned in case of delays.
- **Day 10 — Travel to Cox’s Bazar:** Travel by a suitable domestic connection or overland service. Confirm schedules and allow time for transfers.
- **Days 11–12 — Cox’s Bazar:** Enjoy the waterfront, local food and a coastal excursion if conditions permit. Keep swimming decisions dependent on current beach warnings, tides and local advice.
- **Day 13 — Return to Dhaka:** Return to the capital and stay overnight. If the journey is long or the flight schedule is uncertain, consider moving this return earlier.
- **Day 14 — International departure:** Keep the final day for the flight and airport transfer.

### How to adapt the 14-day route

- **For history lovers:** Replace Cox’s Bazar with a planned heritage route that includes **Bagerhat** or **Paharpur**, but research travel connections and overnight stops first. Both are listed as UNESCO World Heritage properties in Bangladesh. They are not close to each other, so do not assume that both can be visited as a quick side trip from Dhaka.
- **For wildlife-focused travellers:** Replace the coast or northeast section with a properly arranged Sundarbans trip and its required transfers. Keep a buffer after the expedition.
- **For a slower holiday:** Remove one region rather than adding more destinations. Extra nights can make a trip more rewarding by allowing time for local food, markets, community-led experiences and rest.
- **For families or travellers with limited mobility:** Reduce the number of hotel changes and check walking distances, stairs, vehicle access and bathroom facilities before booking.

The 14-day plan is a framework, not a promise that every transfer will fit a particular timetable. Build the final order around confirmed transport and accommodation.

## Itineraries by Travel Interest

### Culture, food and city life

Prioritise Dhaka, with time for Old Dhaka, selected museums, architecture, markets and food experiences. Add Sonargaon if you have enough time for a day trip. Read our [Cultural Etiquette Guide](/plan-your-trip/cultural-etiquette/) before visiting religious sites or arranging visits to local communities.

### Tea gardens, forests and rivers

Combine Srimangal with Sylhet. Choose nature activities based on season and conditions rather than assuming every forest, swamp or river trip is accessible year-round. The Bangladesh Tourism Board lists attractions across the Sylhet region; check the official destination information and local arrangements before travelling.

### Beach and coastal scenery

Base your coastal itinerary around Cox’s Bazar, with optional stops along the coast when access and transport are practical. The weather, sea conditions and local warnings should determine whether swimming or boat activities are appropriate. See the [Cox’s Bazar Travel Guide](/destinations/cox-s-bazar-travel-guide/).

### Wildlife and the Sundarbans

Choose a reputable operator with clear information about the vessel, route, accommodation, inclusions, permits, safety equipment and cancellation arrangements. Wildlife sightings cannot be guaranteed. Follow Forest Department instructions, do not litter, and never leave the group or boat to explore unauthorised areas. Read the [Sundarbans Travel Guide](/destinations/sundarbans-travel-guide/) before booking.

### Archaeology and historic architecture

Consider Dhaka and Sonargaon for a shorter trip. With more time and careful route planning, add the Historic Mosque City of Bagerhat or the Buddhist Vihara at Paharpur. UNESCO identifies these as two of Bangladesh’s World Heritage properties; the Sundarbans is the country’s listed natural World Heritage property. Check the [UNESCO World Heritage Centre’s Bangladesh page](https://whc.unesco.org/en/statesparties/bd){target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} for official site information.

## Should You Include Chattogram or the Chittagong Hill Tracts?

**Chattogram city** can suit travellers interested in museums, historic landmarks, urban life and the waterfront. It may be added to a longer coastal route if transport connections work for your dates. See the [Chattogram (Chittagong) Travel Guide](/destinations/chattogram-travel-guide/).

The **Chittagong Hill Tracts**—including Bandarban, Rangamati and Khagrachhari—are a different region from Chattogram city. Do not treat them as an automatic extension of a beach holiday. As of the research date for this guide, several foreign-government travel advisories warn against or advise reconsidering travel to the Hill Tracts because of security risks. Check your own government’s latest advice and any Bangladesh government access requirements before making plans; insurance may be affected by official travel warnings. If the advice recommends avoiding the region, leave it out of your itinerary.

## Transport Planning: The Part That Makes or Breaks an Itinerary

### Domestic flights

Flights can save time on some long routes, but a flight is not a door-to-door journey. Account for travel to the airport, check-in, security, baggage collection and the transfer to your hotel at the other end. Routes, frequencies, fares and baggage rules change, so verify details for your dates before building the rest of the itinerary around a flight.

Avoid tight connections between a domestic flight and an international departure on separate tickets. An overnight stay in Dhaka is safer when a missed connection would mean losing an expensive international flight.

### Trains

Trains can be a useful option on routes they serve, but journey duration, departure times, classes and availability vary. Consult the official [Bangladesh Railway website](https://railway.gov.bd/){target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} and its linked e-ticketing service. Buy through authorised channels and do not rely on unofficial social-media sellers.

### Buses and private vehicles

Buses and private vehicles can reach places that are not served directly by air or rail, but road travel times are affected by traffic, weather, roadworks, holidays and departure terminal location. Confirm where a service starts and ends, not just the city name on the ticket. For longer trips, consider whether a more expensive transport option is worth the time saved.

See [Transport in Bangladesh](/plan-your-trip/transport-in-bangladesh/) for more detail on choosing and arranging journeys.

## Best Time to Visit Bangladesh for These Routes

For many first-time visitors, **November to February** is a comfortable general period for city sightseeing and several outdoor activities. It is not a universal best season for every destination: water levels, rainfall, heat, sea conditions and wildlife-tour arrangements differ by region.

- **Dhaka and heritage sites:** Cooler months can make walking more comfortable, but check opening days and public holidays.
- **Srimangal and Sylhet:** Rain can affect trails, roads, rivers and swamp excursions. Check conditions locally, particularly during periods of heavy rainfall.
- **Cox’s Bazar:** Sea and beach conditions vary. Follow local warnings and do not assume swimming is safe because other visitors are in the water.
- **Sundarbans:** Choose dates with an operator who can explain the seasonal conditions, permits and exact itinerary. Follow official restrictions.

For a month-by-month overview, read [Best Time to Visit Bangladesh](/plan-your-trip/best-time-to-visit/).

## Budget and Accommodation Planning

A realistic budget depends on your accommodation standard, whether you use domestic flights, how many private transfers you arrange, and whether you book guides or multi-day tours. A route with fewer hotel changes can be more economical and less tiring even if it means visiting fewer places.

When comparing accommodation, check the property's exact location, recent reviews, cancellation policy, taxes and whether airport or station transfers are included. For nature trips, ask what is included in the tour price and what costs extra. Do not assume that the cheapest headline price covers permits, meals, transfers or all activities.

Read [Money and Currency in Bangladesh](/plan-your-trip/money-and-currency/) before arrival. Keep a contingency amount for transport changes, an extra night or an unplanned transfer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How many days are enough to visit Bangladesh?

Five days can provide an introduction to Dhaka and nearby heritage, or a short trip combining Dhaka with one other destination. Seven days gives you more room for one additional region. Ten to fourteen days is better for a varied route with multiple regions, provided you account for travel time and avoid trying to see the entire country in one trip.

### Can I visit Dhaka, Sylhet, Cox’s Bazar and the Sundarbans in seven days?

It is possible to draw such a route on a map, but it is not a comfortable plan for most independent travellers. Each transfer takes time, and the Sundarbans usually requires advance arrangements and a defined boat itinerary. Choose one region outside Dhaka for a seven-day visit, or extend the trip.

### Is it better to travel by domestic flight, train or bus?

It depends on the route, timetable, budget and how much time you have. Flights may save time on longer distances but require airport transfers and buffer time. Trains can be practical where services fit your schedule. Buses and private vehicles provide road access but are vulnerable to traffic and weather delays. Compare the full journey, not just the scheduled travel time.

### Should I return to Dhaka before my international flight?

For most itineraries that include another region, returning to Dhaka the day before an international departure is the safer choice. A same-day connection can be disrupted by delays, and airlines may not protect separate-ticket connections. Follow the connection rules on your ticket and leave enough time for unexpected problems.

### Can I visit the Sundarbans independently?

Do not assume you can enter and explore the forest independently. Access, tickets, vessel arrangements and permitted activities are subject to Forest Department rules. Use official information and a suitable operator, follow instructions from forest staff and do not spend the night inside the forest without the required approval.

### Is it safe to include the Chittagong Hill Tracts?

Safety advice can change, and some foreign-government advisories currently recommend avoiding or reconsidering travel to the Hill Tracts. Check the latest advice from your own government and the relevant Bangladesh authorities before planning. If official advice warns against travel, do not include the region just to complete an itinerary.

### What should I do if rain disrupts my plans?

Keep a flexible day where possible, check local forecasts and transport updates, and follow local instructions around floods, landslides, rivers and beaches. Do not take a boat trip or hike if the operator or local authorities say conditions are unsafe. Avoid scheduling your only chance to reach an international flight immediately after a long road journey.

## Build Your Bangladesh Trip Around Realistic Travel Time

A successful Bangladesh itinerary is not the one with the longest list of places. It is the one that gives you time to experience each stop without spending the whole holiday in transit. Start with your top priority—city culture, tea country, beaches, wildlife or heritage—then add only the destinations that fit the time available.
