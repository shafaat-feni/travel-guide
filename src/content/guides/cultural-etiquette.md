Bangladesh is known globally for its warm hospitality and friendly population. As a predominantly conservative society, understanding local cultural etiquette and social norms helps foreign tourists foster respectful interactions and feel welcome throughout their stay.

## Dress Guidelines for Visitors

Modesty in attire is respected across Bangladesh, particularly outside major international hotel zones and expatriate neighborhoods in Dhaka.

### General Guidelines
* **For Men:** Lightweight long trousers or jeans paired with polo shirts or button-down shirts are standard. Wearing shorts in public is common for sports, but long trousers are preferred when visiting government offices, historic sites, or dining establishments.
* **For Women:** Loose-fitting clothing that covers shoulders, chest, and knees is recommended. Wearing a lightweight scarf (*orna*) over shoulders is common local practice and helpful when visiting traditional markets or religious sites.

## Visiting Religious Sites

Bangladesh features historic Islamic mosques, Hindu temples, Buddhist monasteries, and Christian churches.

### Etiquette at Mosques & Shrines
* **Footwear:** Always remove your shoes before stepping onto carpeted mosque halls or sacred shrine courtyards.
* **Attire:** Men and women should ensure arms and legs are fully covered. Women are requested to cover their head with a scarf when entering active places of worship like [Dhaka's](/destinations/dhaka-travel-guide/) Baitul Mukarram National Mosque or [Sylhet's](/destinations/sylhet-travel-guide/) Hazrat Shah Jalal Shrine.
* **Photography:** Always request permission before photographing people praying or taking pictures inside prayer halls.

## Social Greetings & Handshakes

* **Greetings:** A warm "Assalamu Alaikum" (Peace be upon you) is the standard and widely appreciated greeting across the country.
* **Handshakes:** Conservative social norms govern physical contact between mixed genders. Wait for a local host to extend their hand first for a handshake. A gentle head nod with a hand placed over the heart is a universally polite alternative.
* **Hand Usage:** Use your right hand when giving or receiving objects, money, business cards, or food, as the left hand is traditionally reserved for personal hygiene.

## Guest Hospitality & Dining Customs

* If invited to a local host's home for tea or a meal, accepting with enthusiasm is polite.
* Taking off shoes when entering a residence is standard etiquette across Bangladesh.
