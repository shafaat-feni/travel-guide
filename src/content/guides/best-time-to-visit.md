The **best time to visit Bangladesh for most first-time travellers is usually November to February**, when conditions are generally cooler and drier than during the hot pre-monsoon and rainy seasons. This is a useful starting point for a trip combining cities, beaches, tea-growing areas and wildlife excursions—but the ideal dates depend on what you want to see.

Bangladesh has a subtropical monsoon climate, with regional differences in rainfall and temperature. The Bangladesh Meteorological Department broadly identifies winter as December–February, the hot pre-monsoon season as March–May, the monsoon as June–September and the post-monsoon period as October–November. Travel guides often use slightly different seasonal boundaries because weather changes gradually rather than on a fixed date.

## Quick answer: when should you go?

- **Best all-round window:** November to February for generally drier weather and comfortable sightseeing.
- **Best for Cox's Bazar and coastal trips:** Usually November to March, while checking marine conditions and current travel advice.
- **Best for Sundarbans trips:** Commonly the drier months, often November to February; book with an established operator and check current conditions.
- **Best for lush greenery and waterfalls:** The rainy season can make landscapes especially green, but heavy rain may disrupt transport and outdoor plans.
- **Best for tea gardens:** The cooler, drier months are often easier for walking; the landscape can look especially lush during and after the rains.
- **Best for festivals:** Plan around the specific event dates, which may vary each year. Expect busy transport and accommodation around major national and religious holidays.

There is no month that guarantees perfect weather. Check the forecast shortly before travel, especially if your itinerary includes boats, coastal areas or remote destinations.

## Bangladesh's main seasons

### 1. Cool, generally dry season: November to February

This is the most straightforward season for many international visitors. Days are often mild to warm, humidity is usually lower than in the monsoon, and many outdoor activities are more comfortable. Conditions vary by region, and northern areas can have cool mornings and evenings.

**Best for:**
- Exploring [Dhaka](/destinations/dhaka-travel-guide/) and historical sites.
- Visiting the beaches around [Cox's Bazar](/destinations/cox-s-bazar-travel-guide/) and hill tracts of [Chittagong](/destinations/chittagong-hill-tracts).
- Taking planned boat excursions in the [Sundarbans](/destinations/sundarbans-travel-guide/).
- Walking through tea gardens and countryside around [Srimangal](/destinations/srimangal-travel-guide/) and [Sylhet](/destinations/sylhet-travel-guide/).

**What to keep in mind:** This is a popular travel period, so reserve transport and accommodation in advance for busy dates. Winter fog can sometimes affect road, rail, ferry and flight schedules. Pack layers for cool mornings and evenings, particularly if travelling outside the warmer coastal areas.

### 2. Hot pre-monsoon season: March to May

Temperatures rise during these months, and April and May are often among the hottest. Humidity increases, and afternoon thunderstorms—locally known as *Kalbaishakhi* or Nor'westers—can develop. Rainfall varies across the country, with northeastern areas often receiving substantial pre-monsoon rain.

**Best for:**
- Travellers who can plan sightseeing in the early morning and evening.
- Some cultural visits and city trips, provided you plan around the heat.
- Visitors seeking fewer winter-season crowds, while remaining flexible about weather.

**What to keep in mind:** Heat can make long walks and exposed outdoor sites tiring. Carry water, use sun protection, take breaks in shade and choose accommodation with reliable cooling if you need it. Thunderstorms can disrupt transport, so avoid tight connections and check forecasts before long journeys.

### 3. Monsoon season: June to September

The southwest monsoon brings widespread rain, high humidity and cloudy periods. Much of Bangladesh's annual rainfall occurs during these months, though the amount and timing differ by region. Rivers and wetlands rise, landscapes become lush, and some waterfalls and swamp forests can be particularly impressive.

**Best for:**
- Seeing green countryside and rain-fed landscapes.
- Experiencing seasonal wetlands and waterfalls where access is open and conditions are safe.
- Travellers who have flexible schedules and are comfortable adjusting plans.

**What to keep in mind:** Heavy rain can cause local flooding, waterlogging, landslides in hilly areas and delays to road, rail, boat and air travel. Some excursions may be cancelled or become unsuitable. Coastal weather and river conditions can change quickly; follow official warnings and local operator advice.

If you travel during the monsoon, allow spare days in your itinerary, carry waterproof bags for electronics and documents, and check our [Bangladesh Travel Health Guide](/bangladesh-travel-health/) for advice on heat, insects and staying well.

### 4. Post-monsoon and autumn: October to November

Rainfall often starts to ease during this transition, although October can still bring heavy showers and the risk of tropical weather systems. The landscape may remain green, while conditions gradually become drier as winter approaches.

This shoulder period can suit visitors who want a mix of greenery and improving weather. However, do not assume that every region is dry by October. Check forecasts and warnings, particularly for coastal journeys and boat trips.

## Month-by-month guide

### January
Often cool and dry, making January a strong choice for city sightseeing, beaches and planned nature trips. Mornings can be chilly in some areas, and fog may occasionally affect transport. Reserve popular accommodation and train tickets ahead of busy periods.

### February
Usually part of the comfortable dry season. It can be a good month for a multi-region itinerary, walking tours and outdoor visits. Check the dates of any festivals you want to attend, as crowds and transport demand may increase around events.

### March
Temperatures begin to rise. Outdoor sightseeing is still possible, but schedule walks and visits for earlier in the day and keep water with you. The weather can vary between regions.

### April
Often one of the hottest months, with a chance of strong pre-monsoon thunderstorms. This month may appeal to visitors interested in Pohela Boishakh, the Bengali New Year, but confirm the event programme and prepare for heat and crowds.

### May
Hot, humid conditions and pre-monsoon storms continue, while rainfall may increase in some regions. Keep plans flexible and check forecasts before long road journeys, boat trips or outdoor excursions.

### June
The monsoon is typically underway. Expect high humidity and periods of heavy rain. Lush scenery can be rewarding, but flooding and transport disruption are possible; build flexibility into your itinerary.

### July
Often a very wet month in many parts of Bangladesh. Wetlands and waterways may be full, but travel conditions can be challenging. Check local access, river conditions and weather warnings before committing to excursions.

### August
Rainy conditions often continue. This can be a visually striking time for green landscapes, but outdoor activities may be disrupted by rain, waterlogging or flooding. Avoid assuming that rural roads and trails will be accessible.

### September
Monsoon conditions may persist, although the pattern varies by year and region. Keep checking forecasts and allow spare time for travel. Some areas may remain wet even as rainfall begins to ease elsewhere.

### October
A transition month: conditions may improve, but heavy rain and coastal weather risks can still occur. It can be a quieter time to travel, provided your plans are flexible and you monitor warnings.

### November
Often a good month to visit as rainfall generally decreases and conditions become more comfortable. It is a useful time to start a trip covering several regions, but book popular transport and accommodation ahead of peak dates.

### December
Usually part of the cooler, drier season and a popular month for sightseeing. Book early around holidays and busy weekends. Pack a light layer for cooler evenings and check transport schedules before travel.

## Festivals and public holidays

Festivals can add a memorable cultural dimension to a visit, but they also affect transport, accommodation availability and opening hours.

- **Pohela Boishakh:** Bengali New Year, usually celebrated in mid-April. Expect public events, music, processions and crowds in some areas; prepare for hot weather.
- **Eid-ul-Fitr and Eid-ul-Adha:** Dates follow the Islamic lunar calendar and change each year on the Gregorian calendar. Intercity transport can be extremely busy as people travel to visit family. Book well ahead if your trip overlaps with either holiday.
- **Durga Puja:** Dates vary each year, generally falling in the autumn. Celebrations and public gatherings may affect traffic around venues.
- **Victory Day and other national observances:** Events may be held in cities and at historic sites. Check local schedules and any access restrictions.

Confirm exact dates for your travel year from reliable sources. Avoid assuming that ordinary transport schedules, business hours or site access will remain unchanged during major holidays.

## Weather, safety and what to pack

Whatever month you choose, prepare for Bangladesh's humidity and sudden weather changes.

- **For the dry season:** Bring breathable clothing, sun protection and a light layer for cool evenings or air-conditioned transport.
- **For hot months:** Plan strenuous outdoor activities early, drink safe water regularly and take breaks from direct sun.
- **For monsoon travel:** Pack a lightweight rain jacket, quick-drying clothes, waterproof bags and shoes with good grip.
- **For nature trips:** Use insect repellent and check whether your accommodation or tour recommends additional precautions.
- **For all seasons:** Keep essential medication and travel documents protected, and maintain travel insurance appropriate for your activities.

Before departure and during your stay, consult the <a href="https://bmd.gov.bd/web/en/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Bangladesh Meteorological Department</a> for forecasts and warnings, including heavy rainfall, marine conditions, river-port warnings, heat waves and cyclones. Follow local authorities and tour operators if conditions change.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best month to visit Bangladesh?

For many first-time visitors, November, December, January and February are good choices because conditions are generally cooler and drier. The best month depends on your destinations and activities, and weather can vary from year to year.

### Is Bangladesh worth visiting during the monsoon?

Yes, if you are interested in lush landscapes and seasonal wetlands and are prepared to be flexible. Heavy rain can cause flooding and transport delays, and some excursions may be cancelled. Check current forecasts and local advice before travelling.

### When is the best time to visit Cox's Bazar?

November to March is commonly considered a favourable period for a beach visit. Always check sea conditions and local safety guidance; suitable weather does not guarantee safe swimming.

### When should I visit the Sundarbans?

The cooler, drier months—often November to February—are a common choice for boat-based visits. Availability, routes, permits and conditions vary, so confirm arrangements with a reputable operator before travelling.

### Is summer too hot for Bangladesh?

It can be uncomfortable, especially during the hottest part of the day, but travel is possible with planning. Choose early starts, shade, hydration and suitable accommodation, and be ready to adjust plans during heat or thunderstorms.

### Does it rain all day during the monsoon?

Not necessarily. Rain may come in heavy periods or showers, and patterns differ by location and day. However, even intermittent rain can cause waterlogging or disrupt transport, so plan for possible delays.
