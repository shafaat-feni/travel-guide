Most trips are shaped by the people you meet and the places you explore, but safety conditions can vary by location and change quickly. Before travelling around Bangladesh, check current official advice, keep an eye on local news, and ask your accommodation or a trusted local contact about conditions affecting your planned route.

## Check Official Travel Advice Before You Go

Travel advisories are updated when security conditions, weather events, or local restrictions change. Read the latest advice from your own government shortly before departure and continue checking it during your stay.

**Pay attention to regional warnings, not just the overall country rating.** At the time this page was reviewed on 21 October 2026, the UK FCDO advised against all but essential travel to the Chittagong Hill Tracts districts of Rangamati, Khagrachari, and Bandarban. This advice can change and may differ between governments. Check the current notice and map before planning a visit; do not rely on this page as a live alert.

If your government offers a traveller-registration or alert service, consider enrolling before departure. Save your embassy or high commission's emergency contact details separately from your phone.

## Demonstrations, Political Events and Crowds

Political rallies, protests, strikes, and large gatherings can develop quickly and may cause violence, road closures, or transport disruption. Religious festivals and major public events can also bring dense crowds and extra security measures.

- Avoid demonstrations, political gatherings, and any crowd where tensions appear to be rising.
- If a gathering begins nearby, move away calmly rather than stopping to watch or take photographs.
- Follow instructions from local authorities and security staff, and check local news before travelling across a city.
- Leave extra time for airport transfers and intercity journeys when road closures or public events are reported.

Do not assume that a busy area is automatically unsafe, but avoid unnecessary exposure to a situation you cannot assess.

## Protect Your Belongings and Personal Safety

Petty theft, bag-snatching, scams, and occasional violent crime can occur, especially in crowded transport hubs, markets, and busy urban areas. Keep valuables discreet and stay aware of your surroundings.

- Carry only the cash and cards you need for the day. Keep a backup payment method separate.
- Use a zipped bag and keep your phone secure when standing near traffic or in crowded places.
- Keep your passport, visa details, and insurance information secure. Carry a copy of your passport photo page and relevant visa details for routine identification, while storing the original safely.
- Be cautious if a stranger creates a distraction, insists on carrying your luggage, or pressures you to use a particular driver or service.
- If you think you are being followed, enter a busy, staffed business or hotel and ask for help rather than going somewhere isolated.

Keep digital copies of key documents in a secure account you can access if your phone or bag is lost. Do not store all copies and payment cards in the same place.

## Getting Around More Safely

Road conditions, driving behaviour, traffic density, and vehicle standards vary. Plan journeys with care, particularly after dark or on unfamiliar routes.

- **Ride-hailing and taxis:** Use a reputable service or transport arranged by your accommodation. Match the vehicle registration, model, and driver details to the booking before getting in, and share your trip with someone you trust where possible.
- **Rickshaws and CNG auto-rickshaws:** Agree on the fare beforehand when the service does not use a meter or app. Avoid rides that feel unsafe, and be especially cautious on fast, busy roads.
- **Buses and trains:** Use established operators and official ticket channels. Keep your belongings close and avoid travelling alone overnight where official advice identifies elevated risks.
- **Intercity road travel:** If possible, schedule longer drives during daylight. Allow extra time and avoid vehicles whose driver appears tired or drives aggressively.
- **Boats and ferries:** Check weather and local advice, avoid visibly overcrowded vessels, and do not board if basic safety equipment appears inadequate.

## Solo Travellers and Women Travellers

Solo visitors should plan arrival and late-night transport in advance, keep someone informed of major itinerary changes, and avoid isolated places when they are unfamiliar with the area. Women travellers may experience unwanted attention or harassment in some settings; experiences vary, but it is sensible to have a plan for leaving an uncomfortable situation.

- Arrange airport pickup before landing or use a clearly identifiable, reputable transport service.
- Choose accommodation with recent reviews, a staffed reception, and a location that suits your arrival time.
- Avoid walking alone through isolated areas after dark, and move to a staffed public place if someone makes you feel unsafe.
- You do not owe strangers conversation, photographs, or personal details. A firm, brief refusal and leaving the situation are reasonable responses.
- On public transport, move closer to families, other women, or staff if you feel uncomfortable, and seek help if harassment continues.

These are precautions, not a suggestion that visitors should avoid experiencing local life. Ask trusted local hosts or accommodation staff about the safest practical options for a particular neighbourhood or journey.

## Local Laws and Cultural Considerations

Foreign visitors are subject to Bangladeshi law. Carry appropriate identification, respect instructions from authorities, and do not assume that an activity permitted at home is treated the same way in Bangladesh.

- Keep a secure copy of your passport and visa information available; follow lawful instructions if officials request identification.
- Avoid photographing military, police, border, airport-security, or other restricted sites where photography is prohibited.
- Be respectful around religious sites and public ceremonies. Ask before photographing worshippers or entering private or restricted areas.
- Dress with local expectations in mind, especially at religious sites and in conservative communities. See our [Cultural Etiquette Guide](/plan-your-trip/cultural-etiquette/).
- Avoid illegal drugs and be cautious about carrying medicines that may be controlled or restricted. Check the rules before travel if you use prescription medication.

## Weather, Flooding and Outdoor Trips

Heavy rain, flooding, river conditions, heat, and landslides can affect travel plans. Conditions may change faster than transport schedules or online itineraries suggest.

- Check forecasts and local updates before travelling to coastal, riverine, forested, or hilly areas.
- Do not walk or drive through floodwater; depth, currents, road damage, and contamination may not be visible.
- Follow local guidance about boat trips, forest visits, and restricted areas. Use properly organised guides for remote destinations.
- Keep a flexible itinerary during periods of heavy rain and leave time for delays or cancellations.

## If Your Passport, Phone or Bank Card Is Lost

1. Move to a safe, staffed location and contact your accommodation or a trusted local contact.
2. If a theft has occurred, contact local police and ask how to obtain a report for insurance or replacement documents.
3. Contact your bank or card provider to block missing cards and secure your accounts.
4. Contact your embassy or high commission for advice on emergency travel documents.
5. Notify your travel insurer if the incident may be covered, and keep receipts and copies of reports.
