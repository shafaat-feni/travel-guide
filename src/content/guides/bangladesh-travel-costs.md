# Bangladesh Travel Costs: How Much Should You Budget?

Bangladesh can be an affordable destination for international visitors, but your total trip cost depends heavily on accommodation standards, how often you use private transport, how many domestic flights you take, and whether you join specialist trips such as a Sundarbans cruise. A traveller using local buses and simple guesthouses can spend far less than someone staying in international-standard hotels and hiring a private car and guide.

This guide provides **planning estimates in Bangladeshi taka (BDT, ৳)** to help you estimate the cost of a trip to Bangladesh. They are not fixed tariffs or guaranteed quotes: hotel rates, transport fares, exchange rates and seasonal demand change. Check current prices before booking.

For the best estimate, calculate accommodation, meals and local transport first, then add intercity travel, activities, visa costs, insurance and a contingency fund. International flights to and from Bangladesh are not included in the daily budgets below.

## Quick answer: Is Bangladesh expensive to visit?

For initial planning, a practical starting point is around **৳2,500–4,000 per person per day for budget travel**, **৳5,000–10,000 for a comfortable mid-range trip**, and **৳14,000 or more for higher-comfort travel**. These broad ranges cover ordinary daily expenses; long-distance transport, private multi-day tours, specialist cruises and premium activities may raise the total.

| Travel style | Suggested daily budget per person | What it generally means |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Budget | ৳2,500–4,000 (about US$20–32) | Simple guesthouse, local meals, public transport and modest paid activities |
| Mid-range | ৳5,000–10,000 (about US$40–81) | Comfortable hotel, a mix of local and sit-down restaurants, and regular ride-hailing or private transfers |
| Higher-comfort | ৳14,000–25,000+ (about US$113–202+) | Higher-end hotels, private vehicle or guide, more flexible travel and paid experiences |
| Luxury | Highly variable | Premium hotels, private itineraries, upgraded transport and specialist tours |

USD equivalents are rounded using an illustrative rate of approximately **৳124 per US$1**, close to the Bangladesh Bank rate available when this page was updated. Your card provider, bank or exchange counter may use a different rate and may add fees. Check the latest rate at the [Bangladesh Bank exchange-rate page](https://www.bb.org.bd/en/index.php/econdata/exchangerate) before converting money.

**Important:** These budgets are per person, per day and assume ordinary independent travel. A couple sharing one hotel room may spend less per person on accommodation than a solo traveller. Conversely, a solo traveller hiring a private car or guide may pay nearly the same vehicle cost as a small group.

## 1. Typical prices in Bangladesh

The following ranges are broad planning estimates rather than official price lists. They are intended to help you build a first budget; actual quotes can fall outside them depending on city, quality, season, location and availability.

| Expense | Approximate planning range |
| --- | ---: |
| Simple local meal | ৳150–400 per person |
| Meal at a comfortable restaurant | ৳400–1,000+ per person |
| Tea from a local tea stall | ৳10–30 |
| Bottled drinking water | ৳20–40, depending on size and brand |
| Budget guesthouse or basic room | ৳1,200–2,500 per room per night |
| Comfortable mid-range hotel | ৳3,500–7,000+ per room per night |
| Higher-end or international-standard hotel | Often ৳10,000+ per room per night |
| Short CNG auto-rickshaw journey | Often ৳80–250, but negotiate or confirm the fare first |
| Intercity bus ticket | Roughly ৳400–1,500+ per journey, depending on route and class |
| Domestic flight, one way | Roughly ৳4,000–8,000+ when available at that price |
| Local guide or private vehicle | Obtain a route-specific quote; prices vary substantially |

These figures are not directly comparable in every case. A meal in a tourist-focused restaurant, a hotel near a major attraction, or a last-minute flight can cost substantially more than the lower end of the range. Some accommodation rates may exclude taxes, service charges, breakfast or other fees, so check the final booking total.

### Food and drinking water

Food can be one of the least expensive parts of travelling in Bangladesh if you eat at busy local restaurants and choose everyday Bengali meals. Rice, lentils, vegetables, eggs, fish and chicken dishes are commonly available. Cafés, hotel restaurants and international cuisine generally cost more.

Budget separately for bottled water, snacks, tea, coffee and occasional higher-priced meals. If you have dietary restrictions or prefer familiar international food, allow a larger food budget, particularly outside the biggest cities. Read our [Bangladeshi Food Guide](/experiences/bangladesh-food-guide/) for ideas on what to eat and how to approach local dishes.

### Accommodation

Room prices vary sharply by location and standard. Dhaka has everything from simple budget rooms to premium business hotels. In popular leisure destinations such as Cox’s Bazar, rates may rise during holidays, weekends and busy beach periods. Smaller towns may have fewer hotels that meet international travellers’ expectations, so compare recent reviews and the exact location rather than choosing by price alone.

When comparing hotels, check whether the displayed price includes taxes, breakfast, cancellation flexibility and all service charges.

### Local transport

Rickshaws, CNG auto-rickshaws, ride-hailing services, city buses and hired cars serve different needs. The cheapest option is not always the most practical: traffic can make a short journey take a long time, while a private car may be useful for a full day of sightseeing or a trip to a site outside the city.

Agree on a CNG or rickshaw fare before starting if the vehicle does not use a meter or app fare. For ride-hailing, confirm the destination and price shown in the app. Read [Transport in Bangladesh](/plan-your-trip/transport-in-bangladesh/) for route-planning and transport advice.

### Intercity travel and domestic flights

Long-distance travel can change your total budget more than everyday local spending. Intercity buses and trains are often cheaper than flights, while domestic flights can save time on routes such as Dhaka–Cox’s Bazar or Dhaka–Sylhet when suitable services and fares are available.

Do not budget from a single advertised fare without checking the date, baggage allowance, departure point and final charges. Train ticket availability and fares depend on the route and class; use the official [Bangladesh Railway website](https://railway.gov.bd/){target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"} to access its ticketing service and current notices. For a route-by-route overview, see [Transport in Bangladesh](/plan-your-trip/transport-in-bangladesh/).

## 2. How much does a 7-day trip to Bangladesh cost?

A one-week trip often combines Dhaka with one additional region, such as Cox’s Bazar, Sylhet and Srimangal, or a carefully planned heritage route. The following totals are **illustrative land costs per person**, not package prices. They exclude international flights, visa fees, travel insurance, major shopping and any unusually expensive private expedition.

| Travel style | Estimated 7-day land budget per person | What it assumes |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Budget | ৳17,500–28,000 (about US$140–226) | Basic room, local food, public transport and limited paid activities |
| Mid-range | ৳35,000–70,000 (about US$282–565) | Comfortable accommodation, mixed dining, some ride-hailing or transfers, and ordinary sightseeing |
| Higher-comfort | ৳98,000–175,000+ (about US$790–1,411+) | Higher-end accommodation, private transport and more paid experiences |

The low end assumes careful spending and relatively inexpensive routes. If you add several domestic flights, stay in expensive beach hotels, hire a car for multiple days or book guided activities, the total may exceed the range.

### Example: a 7-day Dhaka and Cox’s Bazar trip

A realistic budget should account for more than the flight ticket between the two destinations:

- **Accommodation:** Seven nights, with the cost depending on how many nights you spend in each city and whether you share a room.
- **Meals and drinks:** Daily breakfast, lunch, dinner, bottled water and snacks.
- **Transport:** Airport transfers, local journeys in Dhaka and Cox’s Bazar, plus transport for any beach excursions.
- **Dhaka–Cox’s Bazar return travel:** Compare the total cost and time of domestic flights with bus options; fares can change considerably by date.
- **Activities:** Entry fees, boat trips, local guides or other excursions you actually plan to take.
- **Contingency:** Extra money for traffic delays, a changed transfer, weather disruption or a more expensive-than-expected meal.

For a first visit, do not plan an international departure immediately after a long road journey. Leave a buffer in Dhaka in case transport is delayed. See the [Dhaka Travel Guide](/destinations/dhaka-travel-guide/) and [Cox’s Bazar Travel Guide](/destinations/cox-s-bazar-travel-guide/) when estimating local activities and accommodation.

## 3. How much does a 14-day trip to Bangladesh cost?

Two weeks gives you time to combine several regions, but it does not automatically cost twice as much as a week. Some costs, such as international airfare and visa fees, are fixed; daily expenses accumulate with each extra night, while longer trips may also require more intercity travel.

| Travel style | Estimated 14-day land budget per person | What it assumes |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Budget | ৳35,000–56,000 (about US$282–452) | Basic accommodation, local food, public transport and a restrained activity budget |
| Mid-range | ৳70,000–140,000 (about US$565–1,129) | Comfortable hotels, restaurant meals, some private transfers and regional travel |
| Higher-comfort | ৳196,000–350,000+ (about US$1,581–2,823+) | Higher-end hotels, more private transport and multiple paid experiences |

These totals are starting estimates, not a promise that a particular itinerary can be completed for the lower figure. A three-day Sundarbans cruise, specialist wildlife tour, private guide, premium beach resort or extensive use of domestic flights should be priced separately before you settle on a budget.

For a longer trip, group nearby destinations geographically. For example, pair Dhaka with Sonargaon; visit Sylhet and Srimangal together where transport connections make sense; or plan a separate southwest route for Khulna, the Sundarbans and Bagerhat. Our [Bangladesh Travel Itineraries](/plan-your-trip/bangladesh-travel-itineraries/) page can help you choose a route that matches your time and spending level.

## 4. What costs extra beyond your daily budget?

The daily ranges above are useful for ordinary travel, but several items need their own line in your budget.

### International flights

Your international airfare may be one of the largest expenses and can vary according to your departure country, season, airline, baggage allowance, stopovers and booking time. Compare the **final return fare**, including baggage and any necessary seat or payment fees, rather than the headline price alone.

### Visa and entry-related expenses

Visa requirements and fees depend on your nationality, application route and circumstances. Check the official requirements before buying non-refundable travel. Read [Bangladesh Tourist Visa Requirements](/plan-your-trip/bangladesh-visa/) for advance-visa and Visa on Arrival guidance.

### Travel insurance and health preparation

Travel insurance is not included in the estimates. Compare coverage for medical treatment, emergency evacuation, trip interruption, baggage and the activities you intend to do. Some specialist trips or remote areas may require extra planning. Review our [Bangladesh Travel Health](/bangladesh-travel-health/) and [Bangladesh Travel Safety](/bangladesh-travel-safety/) guides before departure.

### Guided tours, cruises and permits

A guided excursion may include transport, meals, accommodation, boat hire, permits and other services—or it may charge for each separately. Ask for an itemised written quote and clarify what is excluded. This is especially important for multi-day wildlife trips, the Sundarbans and areas where access rules or local arrangements apply.

### SIM card, data and connectivity

Allow a small separate amount for mobile data, a local SIM or an eSIM plan. Check whether your phone supports the service and whether the package includes enough data for maps, messaging and bookings. See [SIM Cards and eSIMs in Bangladesh](/plan-your-trip/sim-cards-and-esims/).

### Shopping, tips and unexpected costs

Souvenirs, gifts, laundry, snacks, tips where customary, baggage storage and last-minute transfers can add up. Keep a contingency reserve—around **10–15% of your planned land budget** is a useful starting point for ordinary independent travel, though complex itineraries may need more.

## 5. How to keep your Bangladesh trip affordable

You do not need to choose the cheapest option for every part of the trip. Focus on decisions that have the biggest effect on your total spending.

1. **Choose fewer regions rather than rushing across the country.** Every extra destination can add transport fares, transfers and nights in transit. A focused route is often cheaper and more enjoyable.
2. **Use trains or buses when they fit your schedule.** Compare the fare, journey time, station location, baggage and onward transport—not just the ticket price.
3. **Book popular accommodation early when travelling in busy periods.** Check the cancellation policy and final price, including taxes and service charges.
4. **Stay near the places you plan to visit.** A cheaper hotel far from the main sights can cost more in repeated transfers and lost time.
5. **Mix local meals with occasional restaurant meals.** Eat where food is freshly prepared and turnover is high, and choose bottled or otherwise safe drinking water.
6. **Compare the full cost of a private car with multiple separate rides.** For a group or a day trip, a pre-agreed driver may be good value; for one short journey, it may not be.
7. **Check what tours actually include.** Compare permits, guide fees, meals, transfers, accommodation and equipment before deciding which offer is better value.
8. **Avoid tight connections after long road journeys.** A buffer night may cost more upfront but can protect you from missed international flights and expensive last-minute changes.
9. **Carry small notes for everyday purchases.** Keep cash secure and separate, and do not assume every small shop or transport provider accepts cards.
10. **Check the exchange rate and payment fees.** A card or ATM may apply its own exchange rate, foreign-transaction fee or withdrawal fee. Choose whether to pay in BDT or your home currency carefully when a terminal offers a conversion choice.

For currency handling and cash access, see [Money and Currency in Bangladesh](/plan-your-trip/money-and-currency/).

## 6. Cash, cards and exchanging money

The currency is the **Bangladeshi taka (BDT, ৳)**. Keep some cash available for local transport, small eateries, markets and places where card payments may not be accepted. Larger hotels and established businesses may accept cards, but acceptance is not universal and can vary by location.

Before exchanging money, compare the rate and any commission or service charge. Use authorised banks or exchange providers, count the money before leaving the counter, and keep the receipt. Avoid carrying all your travel money in one place.

ATMs can be useful in cities, but do not rely on a single card or assume that every machine will accept an overseas card. Check your bank's international withdrawal fees, tell your bank about travel if required, and carry a backup payment method. For your current conversion estimate, consult [Bangladesh Bank's exchange-rate information](https://www.bb.org.bd/en/index.php/econdata/exchangerate){target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"}.

## 7. How much money should you carry per day?

There is no single cash amount that suits every visitor. A traveller staying in a prepaid hotel and using ride-hailing may need less cash than someone taking local transport, shopping in markets and visiting smaller towns. Carry enough for ordinary small purchases and a backup journey, while keeping the remainder secure.

A practical approach is to:

- Keep a modest amount of small-denomination taka for food, water and local transport.
- Use a card or other secure payment method where accepted.
- Carry a backup card separately from your main wallet.
- Keep emergency funds separate from everyday spending money.
- Avoid displaying large amounts of cash in public.
- Check whether your accommodation, driver or tour operator requires cash payment before you arrive.

Your visa or immigration process may have its own proof-of-funds requirements; those are separate from the daily budget estimates on this page. Check the latest details in our [Bangladesh Visa Guide](/plan-your-trip/bangladesh-visa/).

## 8. Regional differences: where might you spend more?

Costs vary more by service and travel style than by a simple national price ranking, but these patterns can help with planning.

- **Dhaka:** A wide range of hotels and food options is available. Traffic can increase the cost of transfers or private transport, so location matters.
- **Cox’s Bazar:** Beachfront hotels and peak-season dates can be considerably more expensive than basic accommodation elsewhere. Add the cost of excursions and transport along the coast.
- **Sylhet and Srimangal:** You can keep everyday costs moderate, but private transfers, resort stays and organised nature activities raise the budget.
- **Chattogram:** Hotel and transport costs depend on location and standard. If you use the city as a base for onward travel, include transfers and the costs of reaching attractions outside the centre.
- **The Sundarbans:** A multi-day boat expedition is a separate major expense. Compare complete tour packages carefully rather than treating it as an ordinary daily sightseeing cost.
- **Historical sites and smaller towns:** Some entry fees may be modest, but reaching several widely separated sites can make transport the largest cost. Group nearby places into one route.

## 9. Make your own Bangladesh travel budget

Before booking, write down the main costs for your specific trip. Use this simple checklist to avoid leaving major expenses out.

- [ ] International return flight
- [ ] Visa or entry-related fees
- [ ] Accommodation for every night, including taxes and service charges
- [ ] Food, drinking water and snacks
- [ ] Airport transfers and local transport
- [ ] Intercity buses, trains or domestic flights
- [ ] Entry fees, guides, boats and planned activities
- [ ] Travel insurance and health-related preparation
- [ ] SIM or eSIM and data
- [ ] Shopping, tips and other personal spending
- [ ] Emergency reserve and a buffer for delays

Add the costs for all travellers, then divide by the number of people only where an expense is genuinely shared. For example, a hotel room may be shared by two people, but individual entry tickets and meals usually are not.

## Frequently asked questions about Bangladesh travel costs

### Is Bangladesh cheap for tourists?

Bangladesh can be affordable for visitors who use local transport, eat at everyday restaurants and choose simple accommodation. However, international-standard hotels, private cars, domestic flights and specialist tours can make a trip considerably more expensive. The most useful comparison is the total cost of the travel style you want, not a country's reputation for being cheap.

### How much money do I need for one week in Bangladesh?

As an initial land-budget estimate, allow around **৳17,500–28,000 per person for budget travel**, **৳35,000–70,000 for mid-range travel**, or **৳98,000 or more for higher-comfort travel**. These figures exclude international flights, visa fees, insurance and major specialist tours. Adjust them to your route, accommodation and transport choices.

### How much does a two-week trip to Bangladesh cost?

A rough starting point is **৳35,000–56,000 per person for budget travel**, **৳70,000–140,000 for mid-range travel**, and **৳196,000 or more for higher-comfort travel**, excluding international flights and other major extras. A Sundarbans cruise, several domestic flights or premium hotels can increase the total.

### Can I travel around Bangladesh on US$30 a day?

It may be possible with a careful budget, particularly if you use simple accommodation, eat local food and rely on public transport. It becomes harder if you travel alone and pay the full room price, use private transfers, visit expensive resorts or move between distant regions frequently. Treat US$30 as a restrictive budget rather than a guarantee.

### Should I carry US dollars or Bangladeshi taka?

Taka is the local currency and is needed for many everyday purchases. Some authorised providers may exchange major foreign currencies, but the rate and accepted currencies vary. Use authorised exchange services, compare fees and keep enough local cash for small expenses. Do not assume that foreign currency will be accepted directly by ordinary shops or transport providers.

### Are hotels and transport more expensive during holidays?

They can be. Demand often increases around major public holidays, school breaks and popular beach travel periods. Check dates and cancellation terms before booking, and reserve essential intercity transport early when demand is high.

### Does the daily budget include international flights?

No. The daily and sample-trip budgets on this page exclude international flights, visa fees, travel insurance, large shopping expenses and any specialist multi-day expedition not explicitly included. Add those costs separately.
