# Money in Bangladesh: Currency, ATMs, Cards & Cash Guide

Planning how to pay for things in Bangladesh is an important part of preparing for your trip. Although international cards are useful in some hotels, restaurants, shops, and travel businesses, **cash remains essential for many everyday purchases**—including local transport, small eateries, markets, and purchases in rural areas.

For most visitors, a practical approach is to carry a modest amount of Bangladeshi taka for daily spending, keep an international card as a backup, and withdraw or exchange more money through reputable providers when needed. The best option depends on where you are travelling, your bank's fees, and the payment methods accepted by individual businesses.

If you are arriving through Dhaka, read our [Dhaka Airport Arrival Guide](/plan-your-trip/dhaka-airport-arrival-guide/) for arrival planning, and see the [Bangladesh Travel Safety Guide](/bangladesh-travel-safety/) for general precautions when carrying cash.

## Quick money tips for Bangladesh

- **Currency:** The official currency is the Bangladeshi taka (BDT), usually written as ৳.
- **Carry cash:** Keep small notes for local transport, street food, markets, tips, and places that do not accept cards.
- **Keep a backup:** Bring at least one payment card that you have confirmed can be used abroad, and store it separately from your main cash.
- **Check fees:** Your card issuer, the local ATM operator, or a currency-exchange provider may charge fees or apply an exchange-rate margin.
- **Use reputable providers:** Exchange money through banks or licensed money changers, and keep the receipt.
- **Do not rely on one payment method:** Card acceptance, ATM availability, and network service can vary, especially outside major urban areas.

## 1. What currency is used in Bangladesh?

Bangladesh uses the **Bangladeshi taka**, with the currency code **BDT** and symbol **৳**. Prices may be displayed with the symbol or written as an amount followed by “taka” or “BDT.”

Banknotes commonly encountered include ৳5, ৳10, ৳20, ৳50, ৳100, ৳200, ৳500, and ৳1,000. Lower-value notes and coins may also be used, although the denominations you receive in everyday transactions can vary.

### Why you should carry small notes

Small notes are useful for purchases at local shops, food stalls, markets, and short journeys. A driver or small vendor may not have enough change for a large note, particularly early in the day or in a quiet area.

- Keep some ৳20, ৳50, and ৳100 notes handy.
- Use larger notes for bigger purchases where the seller can provide change.
- Count your change discreetly and check it before leaving.
- Avoid displaying a large amount of cash in public.

You do not need to exchange all your money into small notes at once. You can gradually obtain smaller denominations through ordinary purchases or at a bank.

## 2. Should you bring cash or use cards in Bangladesh?

The most reliable strategy for many tourists is to use **both cash and cards**. Cash is useful for small everyday transactions, while an international debit or credit card can be convenient for eligible ATM withdrawals and purchases at businesses that accept it.

| Payment method | Where it is useful | What to keep in mind |
|---|---|---|
| Bangladeshi taka cash | Local transport, small shops, markets, street food, tips, and rural areas | Protect it from loss or theft; keep small notes |
| International debit card | ATM withdrawals and some card payments | Check overseas-use settings, withdrawal limits, and fees |
| International credit card | Some hotels, restaurants, larger shops, and travel bookings | Acceptance varies; some businesses may not accept foreign-issued cards |
| Mobile or digital payments | Certain local services and businesses | Do not assume a foreign visitor can register for every local payment service |
| Foreign cash | Exchanging into BDT at an authorized provider | Check exchange rates, fees, accepted currencies, and declaration requirements |

Do not assume that a business accepts a card just because it is a hotel or restaurant. Confirm before ordering or making a booking, and keep enough cash for essential expenses if a payment terminal or network is unavailable.

## 3. Exchanging foreign currency in Bangladesh

You can exchange eligible foreign cash into Bangladeshi taka through banks and licensed money changers. Availability, opening hours, accepted currencies, and rates vary by location and provider.

### Exchanging money at Dhaka airport

If you arrive at Hazrat Shahjalal International Airport (DAC), exchange counters or bank services may be available in the terminal. Their exact locations, hours, accepted currencies, and rates can change.

For a practical arrival strategy:

1. Check the displayed exchange rate and any commission before handing over your money.
2. Ask how much BDT you will receive after all fees.
3. Count the cash and collect the receipt before leaving the counter.
4. Exchange enough for immediate expenses rather than assuming the airport will offer the best rate for your whole budget.

See our [Dhaka Airport Arrival Guide](/plan-your-trip/dhaka-airport-arrival-guide/) for other tasks to complete after landing.

### Exchanging money in Dhaka and other cities

Banks and licensed money changers in commercial areas may offer exchange services. Use an established provider, check the rate before committing, and request a receipt. Do not exchange money with an unofficial individual offering a seemingly attractive rate on the street.

Before exchanging a large amount, compare the amount you will receive after fees rather than comparing only the advertised rate. Keep the receipt, particularly if you may need evidence of a lawful exchange or may be asked about the source of your taka.

### Which foreign currencies can you exchange?

Major currencies such as US dollars (USD), euros (EUR), and British pounds (GBP) are commonly handled by many exchange providers, but availability is not guaranteed at every counter. Other currencies may be accepted at some locations and not others. If you plan to bring a less commonly traded currency, confirm acceptance before departure.

Bring clean, genuine banknotes in good condition. A provider may decline notes that are damaged, marked, or otherwise unsuitable under its policy.

## 4. Foreign currency declaration rules: what tourists should check

Bangladesh has rules governing the import, declaration, possession, and export of foreign currency. **Do not treat a single dollar threshold as a complete statement of the rules.** Requirements can depend on the amount, currency, method of carriage, and applicable customs or foreign-exchange instructions. Rules and forms can also change.

Before travelling with a substantial amount of cash:

- Check the latest guidance from <a href="https://customs.gov.bd/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Bangladesh Customs</a> and <a href="https://www.bb.org.bd/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Bangladesh Bank</a>.
- Follow the current arrival declaration form and instructions from customs officers.
- Declare cash when required and use the appropriate channel if you have something to declare or are unsure.
- Keep official declaration forms and receipts securely, especially if you intend to take unspent foreign currency out of Bangladesh.
- Do not assume that a threshold mentioned on one form automatically overrides other foreign-exchange or customs requirements.

If you are carrying a large amount, contact the relevant authorities or your airline before departure to clarify the current requirements. The Green Channel should not be used to avoid declaring items or currency that must be declared.

Official resources: <a href="https://customs.gov.bd/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Bangladesh Customs</a> and <a href="https://www.bb.org.bd/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Bangladesh Bank</a>.

## 5. Using international credit and debit cards in Bangladesh

International Visa and Mastercard cards may work at some larger hotels, restaurants, shops, travel companies, and ATMs. Acceptance varies by business, card issuer, card type, and location. American Express and other networks may have more limited acceptance in some places.

Before departure:

- Check with your bank that international transactions and ATM withdrawals are enabled.
- Ask about foreign transaction fees, cash withdrawal fees, exchange rates, and daily limits.
- Check whether you need to notify the bank of travel plans or confirm transactions through its app.
- Make sure you can access the bank's security verification method while abroad.
- Bring a second card if possible and keep it in a separate secure place.

A card that works at a hotel in Dhaka may not work at a small business or in a rural destination. Keep sufficient taka for places where cards are not accepted.

### Choose the right currency if a card terminal offers a choice

If a payment terminal or ATM offers to charge you in your home currency instead of BDT, compare the terms carefully. This is often called dynamic currency conversion. The offered conversion rate may be less favorable than the rate applied by your own bank or card network.

You can generally choose to be charged in the local currency (BDT) and let your card issuer convert the amount, but check your own card's terms and any fees. Decline a conversion only after reading the screen and confirming the amount and currency you are authorizing.

## 6. Withdrawing cash from ATMs in Bangladesh

ATMs are available in major cities and many towns, but availability and reliability vary. International card support is not universal, and an ATM may be temporarily out of service or unable to dispense cash.

Banks with established ATM networks include Dutch-Bangla Bank, BRAC Bank, Eastern Bank, Standard Chartered, and HSBC. This is not a guarantee that every ATM from these banks will accept your particular foreign card. Look for the payment-network symbols displayed on the machine and check the ATM's instructions.

### How to make ATM withdrawals more smoothly

1. **Use a reputable location.** Prefer an ATM attached to a bank branch or in a secure, well-lit location.
2. **Check the network logo.** Make sure the machine supports your card network before starting.
3. **Know your limits.** Your home bank and the local ATM may each set withdrawal limits.
4. **Read the fee screen.** Review any local ATM fee before confirming.
5. **Review the conversion offer.** If the ATM offers to convert the amount into your home currency, compare the terms with your card issuer's exchange arrangements.
6. **Protect your PIN.** Cover the keypad and do not accept help from strangers.
7. **Keep the receipt if needed.** Check your account for the final transaction amount and contact your bank promptly if anything appears incorrect.

### ATM fees and exchange rates

The total cost of withdrawing cash may include a fee charged by the local ATM operator, a fee from your own bank, and a foreign-currency conversion margin. Some accounts reimburse certain fees, while others do not.

Before your trip, ask your bank about its international ATM policy. If you are offered a conversion into your home currency at the ATM, compare it with the option to be charged in BDT. Avoid making repeated small withdrawals if each transaction attracts a fixed fee, but do not withdraw more cash than you can safely carry.

If an ATM retains your card or fails to dispense cash after debiting your account, follow the bank's official instructions and contact your card issuer. Do not share your PIN or banking verification codes with anyone claiming to help.

## 7. How much cash should you carry?

There is no single correct amount for every traveler. Your needs depend on your accommodation, transport choices, planned activities, group size, and access to ATMs or card payments.

A practical approach is to:

- Carry enough taka for immediate transport, food, and small purchases.
- Keep a separate emergency reserve rather than carrying your entire budget in one wallet.
- Store backup cash and your spare card separately.
- Replenish cash through reputable ATMs or exchange providers as needed.
- Carry extra cash when visiting remote areas where ATM access may be limited, while avoiding unnecessary amounts.

If you are travelling outside Dhaka, plan ahead for access to cash. Some destinations may have fewer ATMs or limited card acceptance. ## 8. Is tipping expected in Bangladesh?

Tipping practices vary by setting. A tip is generally a way to show appreciation for good service rather than an automatic requirement for every transaction. Do not feel obliged to tip every person who offers unsolicited help.

### Restaurants and cafés

Check the bill to see whether a service charge has already been added. If a service charge is included, an additional tip is optional. Where no service charge applies, you can leave a modest amount for good service if you wish.

### Hotel staff

Tips for staff who provide helpful services, such as carrying luggage, may be appreciated, but amounts are discretionary. Keep small notes available if you expect to tip.

### Drivers and tour guides

For a private driver or guide, agree on the service price and what it includes before the trip. A tip for good service is optional and can reflect the length, complexity, and quality of the service. For multi-day tours, clarify whether gratuities are included in the quoted price.

## 9. Keeping your money safe

Use the same basic precautions you would use in any unfamiliar destination, especially in busy markets, transport hubs, and crowded public places.

- Keep only the cash you need for the day readily accessible.
- Separate your reserve cash and spare card from your main wallet.
- Use a secure bag and avoid counting a large amount of cash in public.
- Cover your PIN when using an ATM and be alert to people standing unusually close.
- Use bank branches or reputable exchange counters rather than informal street exchanges.
- Keep transaction receipts for large exchanges and report suspicious card activity to your bank.
- Store emergency bank contact details somewhere accessible if your phone is lost.
- Do not give your card, PIN, password, or one-time verification code to another person.

If your wallet or card is lost, contact your bank or card issuer immediately to freeze the card, review recent transactions, and arrange a replacement if possible. If theft occurs, consider reporting it to local police and follow your travel insurer's claims process.

## 10. Common money questions for tourists in Bangladesh

### Can I use US dollars or euros directly in Bangladesh?

Do not assume foreign cash will be accepted for ordinary purchases. The local currency is the Bangladeshi taka (BDT), and many everyday transactions are made in taka. Exchange foreign cash through a reputable provider and check the rules before carrying a large amount.

### Are international credit cards accepted in Bangladesh?

Some larger hotels, restaurants, shops, and travel businesses accept international cards, but acceptance is not universal. Carry cash for small purchases and locations where card payments are unavailable.

### Will my foreign debit card work at Bangladeshi ATMs?

It may work if the card network is supported and your bank permits international withdrawals. Check your bank's rules, fees, and limits before departure, and look for the network logo on the ATM.

### Is it better to exchange money at the airport or in the city?

Airport exchange services can be convenient for immediate expenses, but rates and availability vary. Compare the amount you receive after fees and exchange only what you need at first if you prefer to compare options later.

### Should I carry a lot of cash when travelling outside Dhaka?

Plan for limited ATM access and cash-based businesses in some areas, but avoid carrying your entire travel budget. Estimate likely expenses, keep a reserve securely separated, and check destination-specific arrangements before travelling.

### Can I bring foreign cash into Bangladesh?

Foreign-currency import and declaration rules apply. Check the current requirements from Bangladesh Customs and Bangladesh Bank, declare funds when required, and keep any official declaration or exchange receipts.
